Age-banded rubric  ·  How to read it

Ten yes-or-no checks. Each one passed is worth 10 points.

Every product and every recipe gets one score from 0 to 100. Each of the ten checks is a plain yes (1) or no (0), and the score is the number passed times 10, so a product that passes everything scores exactly 100 and one that passes nothing scores 0. Six of them are numbers off the label, measured as a share of the daily reference for the age group (and, from 10 years, the sex and kind of work): added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre and protein. Fibre and protein are credit metrics (more is better); the others are risk metrics (less is better). Three more are checks that do not change with age: preservatives, artificial additives and base-ingredient integrity. The tenth is HFSS, which fails if the sugar, sodium or saturated-fat check fails. A product that fails any one risk check is held at OK: the score still shows the real count, with a "capped" badge. Packs and recipes are scored the same way, per realistic serving, so they can be compared. The same method is used on the Products and Meal Ideas pages. Every number below is cited, and the sources are listed at the bottom of this page.

Step 1

Pick an age band

The age groups are the ones the ICMR-NIN 2020 table uses: 1–3, 4–6 and 7–9 years; boys and girls at 10–12, 13–15 and 16–18; and adults, men and women at sedentary, moderate or heavy work. Under 12 months we use the 1–3 year reference. Tap a group to see its reference numbers. Pregnant and lactating women are not scored yet.

Under 12 months there is no published reference band, so the 1–3 years reference numbers are used. Only plain foods meant for babies are scored: a product with added sugar or salt is shown as “Not for under 1”, not as a number.

References

Every number on this page, sourced

  1. [1] ICMR-NIN, "Nutrient Requirements for Indians — Recommended Dietary Allowances and Estimated Average Requirements," 2020. Source for the energy requirement, protein RDA and fibre RDA of every age group on this page, including boys and girls from 10 to 18 and men and women by kind of work.
  2. [2] WHO, "Guideline: Sugars intake for adults and children," 2015. Free sugars should be under 10% of total energy, with additional benefit below 5% — converted to grams here at 4 kcal/g.
  3. [3] WHO saturated fat guidance (WHO/FAO expert consultations on fats and fatty acids). Saturated fat under 10% of total energy — converted to grams here at 9 kcal/g.
  4. [4] Sodium: ICMR-NIN 2020, with the WHO method for scaling. ICMR-NIN 2020 gives an adult sodium limit of 2,000 mg a day and no child figure, so the child limits here scale that adult figure by each age band's energy (the WHO method). Fibre comes from ICMR-NIN 2020 directly.
  5. [5] FSSAI, Food Safety and Standards (Prohibition and Restrictions on Sales) Regulations. Trans fat capped at ≤2% of total oils and fats in any food product, all ages, all categories.
  6. [6] FSSAI, Food Safety and Standards (Food Products Standards and Food Additives) Regulations, 2011. Governs permitted preservatives (Class II) and additives (colours, flavour enhancers) by INS number.
  7. [7] FSSAI compliance affidavit, Supreme Court of India, W.P.(C) No. 437/2024 — 3S and Our Health Society vs Union of India, filed 29 August 2026. Proposes a front-of-pack red hexagon warning for foods high in added saturated fat, added sugar, and/or salt (HFSS), with thresholds to be drawn from the Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2024 (ICMR-NIN). This is a live, pending proceeding — not yet a notified regulation.

What is a citation and what is a judgment call: metrics 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 10 are numbers compared against a published daily reference. Metrics 4, 7 and 8 are declared or not-declared checks against a named regulation. Metric 9 (base-ingredient integrity) is the one metric without a single governing regulation. It is our own read of ingredient order, applied the same way every time. Under 12 months there is no published reference band, so the 1 to 3 year reference is used and the page says so.
