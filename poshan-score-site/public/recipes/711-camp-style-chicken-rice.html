Camp Style Chicken Rice

Recipe by Roti n Rice

Photo: Roti n Rice
⏱ 45 minServes 2Non-vegetarianLunchFrom 1 years

Camp Style Chicken Rice scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Go easy on the chilli for under-5s: use less, or leave it out.

Ingredients

  • 1 ½ cups long grain rice (300g)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 inch ginger (peeled and julienned)
  • 2 cloves garlic (minced)
  • 6 oz boneless, skinless chicken breast ((cut into thin slices) (170g))
  • 1/8 tsp salt (or to taste)
  • ¼ tsp ground pepper
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 cups water (480ml)
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tbsp dark soy sauce
  • 1 red chili ((finely sliced for garnishing) (optional))
  • 2 green onions ((finely sliced for garnishing) (optional))

Method

  • Rinse and drain long grain rice with as little water as possible. Do this twice.
  • In a pot, heat olive oil. Sauté julienned ginger and minced garlic until fragrant, about 1 minute over low heat.
  • Add chicken, salt, ground pepper, and increase heat to medium. Stir fry for a couple of minutes.
  • Add rice and soy sauce. Stir to mix well.
  • Pour in water, cover, and bring to a boil.
  • Reduce heat to medium low, and allow it to simmer until all water is absorbed, about 10 to 12 minutes.
  • Turn heat down to the lowest setting, and continue to cook for another 5 minutes. Turn off heat and let it sit for another 5 minutes.
  • Drizzle sesame oil and dark soy sauce over cooked rice. Gently stir to mix.
  • Dish onto plates and sprinkle with sliced red chili and green onions, if using.
  • Serve immediately.

    Common questions

    Is Camp Style Chicken Rice healthy for kids?

    Camp Style Chicken Rice scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on sodium, fibre, hfss status.

    What age is Camp Style Chicken Rice best for?

    It scores highest, 80 out of 100, for 1–3 years, and lowest, 70, for 4–6 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

    How is the Poshan Score worked out?

    Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
