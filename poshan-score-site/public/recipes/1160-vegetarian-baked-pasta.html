Vegetarian Baked Pasta

Recipe by Vege Home Cooking

Photo: Vege Home Cooking
⏱ 55 minServes 6VegetarianDinnerLunchFrom 2 years

Vegetarian Baked Pasta scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Go easy on the chilli for under-5s: use less, or leave it out.

Ingredients

  • 350 g dried pasta such as rigatoni, ziti, or penne
  • Water to cook pasta
  • Salt
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 celery rib, finely chopped
  • 1 medium zucchini, diced
  • 1 carrot, peeled and diced
  • 1 bell pepper, orange or yellow, cored and diced
  • Salt to taste
  • ½ tsp sweet paprika
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 4 cups marinara sauce (add more if the pasta mixture looks dry)
  • 1 cup ricotta cheese
  • ½ cup shredded cheddar cheese
  • ½ cup shredded mozzarella cheese
  • ½ cup mozzarella cheese (or as needed)
  • 6-8 fresh basil leaves torn or chopped
  • Dried oregano to sprinkle
  • Red pepper flakes to sprinkle (optional)

Method

  • Preheat the oven to 375°F (190°C) and lightly oil a 9x13-inch baking pan.

    • Prepare The Ricotta Mixture

    1. In a medium bowl, combine the ricotta cheese, shredded cheddar, and shredded mozzarella cheese.
    2. Mix everything well and set aside while preparing the pasta and vegetables.

    Common questions

    Is Vegetarian Baked Pasta healthy for kids?

    Vegetarian Baked Pasta scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on sodium, saturated fat, hfss status.

    What age is Vegetarian Baked Pasta best for?

    It scores highest, 70 out of 100, for 1–3 years and 4–6 years and 7–9 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

    How is the Poshan Score worked out?

    Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
