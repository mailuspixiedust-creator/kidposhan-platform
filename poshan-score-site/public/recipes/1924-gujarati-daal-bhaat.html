Gujarati daal bhaat

Recipe by Nehas Cook Book

Photo: Nehas Cook Book
⏱ 35 minServes 3VegetarianDinnerLunchSchool lunchboxFrom 3 years

Gujarati daal bhaat scores 80 out of 100 (OK, held by a risk check) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Go easy on the chilli for under-5s: use less, or leave it out.

Ingredients

  • ½ cup toor daal
  • 1.5 cup + ½ cup water
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • 2 dry red chilli
  • ½ tsp ginger
  • ½ cup chopped tomatoes
  • 30 grm or ¼ cup chopped green garlic
  • ¼ + ¼ tsp turmeric powder
  • Salt to taste
  • 1 tsp red chilli powder
  • 1 tbsp jaggery
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • Some coriander leaves
  • 1 cup soaked rice
  • 1.5 cup water
  • ¼ tsp salt
  • 1 tsp oil
  • ¼ cup green peas (optional)

Method

For green garlic daal

  1. Wash the toor dal and rice with water till water is no more cloudy.
  2. Take daal into the pressure cooker along with 1 ½ cups of water, turmeric powder, salt, and oil.
  3. Cover with the lid and cook daal for 4-5 whistles on medium flame or till the dal becomes soft.
  4. Meanwhile, chop tomatoes and green garlic into small pieces.
  5. Let the pressure go down by itself then open the lid.
  6. Using a hand blender, mash the dal and make it smooth.
  7. Add the required water and again turn the heat on low and let it simmer.
  8. heat the oil in a tadka pan. Once hot, add dried red chili and cumin seeds, and ginger paste and cook for few seconds.
  9. Then add chopped tomatoes and fresh garlic. Add turmeric powder, salt and chili powder, and mix.
  10. Cook till tomatoes become a little soft and oil starts to leave the sides.
  11. Add this tadka to the dal. Stir it and let it simmer for 5 minutes.
  12. Then add jaggery and garam masala. Mix well.
  13. Switch off the gas; add lemon juice and coriander leaves.
  14. Serve green garlic-flavored daal with steamed rice.

How is the Poshan Score worked out?

Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
