Black Pepper Chicken

Ingredients

  • 500 gms boneless chicken thighs / cut into ¾ inch cubes
  • 1 tablespoon + 1 teaspoon light soy sauce
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • ½ teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon crushed pepper
  • 8 garlic cloves grated
  • 2 teaspoons cornflour
  • 2 teaspoons all purpose flour (maida)
  • 2 teaspoons ground pepper
  • 1 teaspoon sichuan pepper powder
  • 2 tablespoons honey
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • ¼ cup chicken stock
  • ½ teaspoon cornflour mixed in 2 tablespoons water
  • 3 tablespoons sunflower oil or any neutral flavoured oil (divided)
  • 2 teaspoons finely chopped garlic
  • 2 teaspoons finely chopped ginger
  • 4 green chillies (slit into 2)
  • ½ cup celery stalks (sliced diagonally into ½ inch length pieces)
  • 2 small onions (cut into petals, approx. 1 cup)
  • ½ cup red bell pepper cut into 1 inch squares
  • 2 tablespoons spring onion greens (finely chopped)

Common questions

Is Black Pepper Chicken healthy for kids?

Black Pepper Chicken scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on added sugar, sodium, being high in fat, sugar or salt.

What age is Black Pepper Chicken best for?

It scores highest, 70 out of 100, for 1–3 years and 4–6 years and 7–9 years, and lowest, 60, for 10–12 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

How is the Poshan Score worked out?

Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
