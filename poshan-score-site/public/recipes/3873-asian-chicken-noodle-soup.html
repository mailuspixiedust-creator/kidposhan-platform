Ingredients

Tick what you have at home and what you need to buy. We keep your ticks on this device.
  • 4 ounces Asian noodles (see notes): such as soba noodles, ramen noodles, udon noodles, or rice noodles
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 2 fat shallots, diced
  • 1 tablespoon ginger, peeled and finely minced or grated
  • 2 garlic cloves, grated or minced
  • 8 oz shiitake mushrooms, sliced, stems removed (or other mushrooms)
  • 6 cups chicken stock
  • 2 cups bok choy, chopped (or other wilting greens)
  • 2 cups snow peas, cut into bite-sized pieces
  • 1 pound chicken breasts, sliced into very thin strips about 1/8″ thick, cut against the grain. Season with a sprinkle of salt.
  • 1 teaspoon sriracha sauce, more to taste
  • 1 1/2 tablespoons toasted sesame oil — adjust to taste
  • 1/4 cup white miso paste (mix with a little hot broth first before adding to the soup)
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce or GF liquid aminos
  • Optional: ½ teaspoon hondashi granules- see notes
  • Optional: A few drops of rice vinegar
  • Optional garnishes: 4 scallions (sliced at a diagonal), toasted sesame seeds, crispy shallots, chili crisp or sriracha

Method

  • Cook noodles separately according to the package directions, then rinse thoroughly under cold water to remove excess starch.
  • Toss with a little oil to prevent sticking. (See notes about cooking directly in the broth)
  • In a heavy pot or Dutch oven heat to medium and add 1 tablespoon oil.
  • Sauté shallots, ginger, and mushrooms over medium heat until the shallots are tender and fragrant.
  • Add the stock and bring to a boil.
  • If cooking noodles directly in the broth, add them in now. (Note that the veggies and chicken will only take 3-4 minutes; adjust cooking time accordingly.)
  • Thinly slice the chicken breast against the grain into 1/8-inch strips and season lightly with salt.
  • Add the chicken, bok choy, and snow peas to the simmering broth.
  • Cook for 4 minutes, or until the vegetables are tender-crisp and the chicken reaches 165°F (74°C).
  • Turn off the heat.
  • Stir the miso paste into a small bowl of hot broth until smooth, then add it back to the pot along with the sriracha, toasted sesame oil, and optional hondashi granules.
  • Taste and adjust with soy sauce or additional miso for saltiness, sriracha for heat, and a splash of rice vinegar for brightness.
  • .
  • If cooked separately, add the noodles to each bowl and ladle the hot soup over them.
  • Sprinkle with scallions and toasted sesame seeds and serve with optional chili crisp or crispy shallots.
