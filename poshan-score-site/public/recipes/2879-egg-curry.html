Egg Curry

Ingredients

Tick what you have at home and what you need to buy. We keep your ticks on this device.
  • 8 pieces large eggs
  • 4 tablespoons cooking oil
  • 1 piece large potato (peeled and cut into cubes)
  • 1 piece medium carrot (peeled and sliced)
  • 1 piece medium yellow onion (chopped)
  • 4 cloves garlic (minced)
  • 1 thumb ginger (cut into thin strips)
  • 1 teaspoon fish sauce
  • 1 tablespoon yellow curry powder
  • 250 ml Nestle All-Purpose Cream
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1 piece medium red bell pepper (sliced)
  • 2 pieces long green peppers
  • 6 grams Maggi Magic Sarap

Method

  • Boil the eggs for 9 to 10 minutes. Transfer them to a bowl of cold water and let them cool. Peel the eggs and set them aside.
  • Heat the cooking oil in a pan.
  • Add the potato and cook for 3 minutes.
  • Add the carrot and continue cooking for 2 minutes.
  • Remove the vegetables from the pan and set them aside.
  • Using the same pan, sauté the onion for 2 minutes.
  • Add the garlic and ginger. Continue sautéing for 1 minute.
  • Add the fish sauce and cook for 30 seconds.
  • Add the curry powder and stir for 20 seconds.
  • Pour in Nestle All-Purpose Cream and water. Stir and let the mixture come to a gentle simmer.
  • Return the potato and carrot to the pan. Cover and cook over low to medium heat for 3 to 5 minutes, or until the potato becomes tender.
  • Add the red bell pepper, long green peppers. Gently stir and simmer uncovered for 3 minutes.
  • Add the boiled eggs and season with Maggi Magic Sarap.
  • Transfer to a serving plate. Serve with warm rice. Share and enjoy!

    Common questions

    Is Egg Curry healthy for kids?

    Egg Curry scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on saturated fat, fibre, being high in fat, sugar or salt.

    What age is Egg Curry best for?

    It scores highest, 70 out of 100, for 1–3 years and 4–6 years, and lowest, 60, for 7–9 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

    How is the Poshan Score worked out?

    Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
