Ingredients

  • 200 g paneer (cut into cubes)
  • 2 tsp tomato ketchup
  • 1 tsp red chili sauce
  • 1 tsp soy sauce
  • A pinch of black pepper powder
  • ¼ tsp salt
  • 2 tsp cornstarch
  • 2 tbsp oil (for stir-frying the paneer)
  • 1 tsp oil (if required)
  • ½ tbsp finely chopped ginger
  • ½ cup finely chopped green beans
  • ½ cup finely chopped carrots
  • ¼ cup corn kernels
  • ½ cup finely chopped green peppers
  • ¼ cup finely chopped cabbage
  • Salt to taste
  • Black pepper powder to taste
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tsp Jain Schezwan sauce (or add hot sauce)
  • 3 cups cooked and cooled basmati rice
  • Stir-fried paneer cubes
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped coriander leaves

Method

Coat the Paneer

  1. In a medium mixing bowl, combine the tomato ketchup, red chili sauce, soy sauce, black pepper, salt, and cornstarch. Mix until smooth.
  2. Add the paneer cubes and gently toss to coat evenly.

Common questions

Is Paneer Fried Rice (No Onion No Garlic) healthy for kids?

Paneer Fried Rice (No Onion No Garlic) scores 60 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 6 of the 10 checks and loses points on sodium, saturated fat, fibre, being high in fat, sugar or salt.

What age is Paneer Fried Rice (No Onion No Garlic) best for?

It scores highest, 90 out of 100, for 13–15 years and 16–18 years, and lowest, 60, for 1–3 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

How is the Poshan Score worked out?

Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
