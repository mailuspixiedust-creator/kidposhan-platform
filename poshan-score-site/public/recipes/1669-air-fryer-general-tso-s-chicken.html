Air Fryer General Tso's Chicken

Air Fryer General Tso's Chicken scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Go easy on the chilli for under-5s: use less, or leave it out.

Ingredients

  • 500 gms boneless chicken thigh / cut into ¾ inch cubes
  • 2 teaspoons grated ginger
  • 1 tablespoon grated garlic
  • 2 tablespoons cornflour
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • 2 egg whites
  • 1 ½ cups all purpose flour (maida)
  • 1 ½ teaspoons baking powder
  • 1 tablespoon water
  • ¼ cup light soy sauce
  • 2 teaspoons dark soy sauce
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 tablespoons vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons ketchup
  • 1 teaspoon white pepper
  • 2 teaspoons cornstarch
  • ½ cup water
  • 2 teaspoons toasted sesame oil
  • 1 tablespoon cooking oil
  • 1 tablespoon garlic (finely chopped)
  • 1 tablespoon ginger (finely chopped)
  • 6 red chillies (whole)
  • ½ red capsicum (cut into ½ inch squares)
  • 1-2 tablespoons cooking oil for brushing (additional oil if deep frying)

Method

Marinating chicken

  1. Take all ingredients for marination in a bowl, mix to combine well. Set aside for 30 minutes.

Common questions

Is Air Fryer General Tso's Chicken healthy for kids?

Air Fryer General Tso's Chicken scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on added sugar, sodium, hfss status.

What age is Air Fryer General Tso's Chicken best for?

It scores highest, 70 out of 100, for 1–3 years and 4–6 years and 10–12 years, and lowest, 60, for 7–9 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

How is the Poshan Score worked out?

Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
