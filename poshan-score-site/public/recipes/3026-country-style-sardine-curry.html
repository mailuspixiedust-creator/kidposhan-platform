Country Style Sardine Curry

Recipe by ruchikrandhap.com

Photo: ruchikrandhap.com
⏱ 25 minNon-vegetarianDinnerLunchFrom 1 years

How often: Sometimes · high in saturated fat About once a week. The Poshan Score shows how one serving does against a child's daily limits; this line is about how the food is made.

Country Style Sardine Curry scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Ingredients

  • 500 grams 12 small sardines
  • 2 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1/4 teaspoon fenugreek (methi seeds or 2 fat pinches of methi seed powder)
  • 1 medium onion (chopped)
  • 1/2 inch piece of ginger (minced)
  • 3 flakes of dried kokum
  • 1 sprig curry leaves (finely chopped)
  • 3/4 cup coconut
  • 2-3 long dry red chillies (preferably byadge)

Method

  • Clean the fish and wash it in a couple of changes of water. Keep on a colander for a few minutes to drain off excess water
  • Fry the methi seeds in a few drops of coconut (or any other) oil and powder them a mortar & pestle. Skip this step if you are using methi powder
  • Using 2-3 tablespoons of water, grind the coconut & the red chillies to a slightly coarse paste (the texture of the masala should be finer than that of coconut chutney but not as fine as regular coconut curry paste).
  • Remove the paste into a bowl and rinse the mixer jar with 1 cup of water
  • Add the chopped ingredients (onions, ginger, kokum & curry leaves) to the bowl of ground masala and mix well. Divide the masala into 3 parts
  • Layer a heavy based pan/kadai or an earthen vessel with one part of the masala.
  • Place 4 sardines over it.
  • Place another layer of the masala and top it with a few more sardines.
  • The final layer will have the last portion of the masala and the last few sardines.
  • Pour the reserved masala water over it and add a little more depending on how much gravy you need.
  • Don't add too much or it will be diluted.
  • Add salt to taste and bring the curry to a boil.
  • Cover and cook for approx 2 minutes.
  • Remember not to overcook it as the fish is added before the curry begins to boil.
  • Overcooking will disintegrate the flesh & you will have too many bones to deal with.
  • Reduce the heat to a simmer, pour the coconut oil and sprinkle the powdered methi seeds over it.
  • Cook for half a minute (or an extra minute if the sardines are big).
  • Remove from heat and let the pan remain uncovered for half a minute.
  • Serve piping hot with red boiled rice or white rice

    Common questions

    Is Country Style Sardine Curry healthy for kids?

    Country Style Sardine Curry scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on saturated fat, fibre, being high in fat, sugar or salt.

    What age is Country Style Sardine Curry best for?

    It scores highest, 90 out of 100, for 7–9 years and 10–12 years and 13–15 years, and lowest, 70, for 1–3 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

    How is the Poshan Score worked out?

    Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
