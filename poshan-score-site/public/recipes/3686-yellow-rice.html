Ingredients

Tick what you have at home and what you need to buy. We keep your ticks on this device.
  • 3 cloves
  • 2 cardamoms (bruised)
  • 1 inch stick of cinnamon or cassia bark
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 medium sized onion finely sliced
  • 2 cups basmati rice
  • 4 cups vegetable or chicken stock or plain water (freshly boiled)
  • 2 bouillon cubes (if stock is not used)
  • 2 tablespoons lime juice or 1 medium big tomato sliced
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder
  • ghee or oil
  • salt to taste

Method

  • Wash and soak the rice in plenty of water for about 10-15 minutes. Then drain on a colander.
  • In a pan heat the ghee and fry the whole spices - cloves, cardamom, cinnamon and bay leaf on a medium heat.
  • Add the sliced onions and fry till they turn translucent.
  • If you are using the tomatoes, add them now and fry until they turn limp.
  • Add the drained rice and fry until the ladle begins to feel heavy. If you fry beyond this point the rice will begin to break.
  • Add the freshly boiled stock (or water and the bouillon cubes dissolved in it) and adjust salt to taste.
  • Add the lime juice if tomatoes were not used.
  • Add the turmeric powder and mix well.
  • Bring the mixture to a rolling boil, then completely reduce heat to a simmer.
  • Cover the pan well with aluminium foil and/or a well fitting lid so that no steam can escape.
  • Set a timer for 5 minutes and let the rice cook on the low heat.
  • After 5 minutes are up turn off the heat and without opening the pan at all set another timer for 15 minutes. The rice will cook in its steam.
  • After 15 minutes are up open the pan and gently fluff up the rice using a fork.
  • Cover for half a minute again and then serve hot with veg or non veg curry and/or raitha

    Common questions

    Is Yellow Rice healthy for kids?

    Yellow Rice scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on sodium, saturated fat, being high in fat, sugar or salt.

    What age is Yellow Rice best for?

    It scores highest, 70 out of 100, for 1–3 years and 4–6 years and 7–9 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

    How is the Poshan Score worked out?

    Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
