Lemon Rice

Recipe by Awesome Cuisine / Praveen Kumar

Photo: Awesome Cuisine / Praveen Kumar
⏱ 30 minVegetarianBreakfastDinnerLunch4 PM snackFrom 2 years

How often: Often A few times a week. The Poshan Score shows how one serving does against a child's daily limits; this line is about how the food is made.

Lemon Rice scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Go easy on the chilli for under-5s: use less, or leave it out.

Ingredients

Tick what you have at home and what you need to buy. We keep your ticks on this device.
  • 1 cup Pachai Arisi (Raw Rice / Extra Long-Grained Rice) (cooked and set aside to cool)
  • 1/4 cup Lemon Juice
  • 1 tsp Salt
  • 1/4 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 2 Green Chillies (finely chopped)
  • 1 tbsp Fresh Ginger (minced)
  • 3 tbsp Peanuts (roasted)
  • 2 tbsp Oil
  • 1 Dry Red Chilli
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetida Powder
  • 3 Curry Leaves
  • 1 tsp Black Mustard Seeds
  • 1 tsp Urad Dal
  • 1 tsp Chana Dal

Method

  • Combine the lemon juice, salt and turmeric powder in a bowl.
  • Set aside.
  • Put the oil in a frying pan over medium heat.
  • When the oil is hot add the dry red chilli, asafoetida powder and curry leaves.
  • Stir in the mustard seeds, urad dal and chana dal.
  • Fry till mustard seeds splutter and the dals are golden for about 30 seconds.
  • Add the cooked rice.
  • Stir in the lemon juice mix.
  • Let it simmer for a few minutes.
  • Remove from heat.
  • Add green chillies, ginger and peanuts.
  • Toss gently for the ingredients to blend and serve.

    Common questions

    Is Lemon Rice healthy for kids?

    Lemon Rice scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on sodium, fibre, being high in fat, sugar or salt.

    What age is Lemon Rice best for?

    It scores highest, 90 out of 100, for 10–12 years, and lowest, 70, for 4–6 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

    How is the Poshan Score worked out?

    Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
