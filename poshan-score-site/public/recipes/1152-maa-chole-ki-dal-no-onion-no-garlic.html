Maa Chole Ki Dal (No Onion No Garlic)

Maa Chole Ki Dal (No Onion No Garlic) scores 80 out of 100 (OK, held by a risk check) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Go easy on the chilli for under-5s: use less, or leave it out.

Ingredients

  • ¾ cup whole black urad dal (whole black gram lentils)
  • ¼ cup Chana dal (split chickpeas)
  • Salt to taste
  • ¼ tsp turmeric powder
  • 1 - inch ginger peeled
  • 1-2 green chilis
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 2 ½ cups water
  • 2 tbsp ghee (or oil for a vegan diet)
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • ¼ tsp asafoetida/Hing
  • ½ - inch ginger peeled and finely chopped
  • 1 large tomato finely chopped
  • ¼ tsp garam masala or to taste
  • 2 tsp coriander powder
  • ½ tsp red chili powder or to taste
  • ½ tsp Kasoori Methi/dried fenugreek leaves
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped coriander leaves

Method

Preparations

  1. Wash urad dal and chana dal thoroughly until water runs clear. Then, soak them together in water for at least 8 hours or overnight.

Common questions

Is Maa Chole Ki Dal (No Onion No Garlic) healthy for kids?

Maa Chole Ki Dal (No Onion No Garlic) scores 80 out of 100 (OK, held by a risk check) for 4–6 years. It passes 8 of the 10 checks and loses points on saturated fat, hfss status.

What age is Maa Chole Ki Dal (No Onion No Garlic) best for?

It scores highest, 100 out of 100, for 7–9 years and 10–12 years and 13–15 years, and lowest, 80, for 1–3 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

How is the Poshan Score worked out?

Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
