Thali

Recipe by Nehas Cook Book

Thali scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Go easy on the chilli for under-5s: use less, or leave it out.

Ingredients

  • 150 grm methi leaves (3 cup chopped methi leaves)
  • 5 tbsp oil
  • ½ tsp mustard seeds
  • ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • Pinch of hing
  • 1 tsp ginger paste
  • 1 tbsp garlic paste
  • 1 tbsp green chilli paste
  • 1 medium size chopped tomatoes
  • Salt to taste
  • ½ tsp turmeric powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • 1 tsp red chilli powder
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • ½ tsp dry mango powder
  • 1 tsp sugar (optional)
  • ¾ cup gram flour (besan)
  • 2 cup hot water
  • 1 cup water or as required
  • 2 cup bajra flour
  • Ghee for serving

Method

Making methi nu lotiyu

  1. Wash and chop methi leaves. Do not chop it finely. Keep it aside.
  2. In a pan, add oil, mustard seeds, cumin seeds, and hing. Saute it.
  3. Then add ginger paste, garlic paste, and green chili paste. Saute it.
  4. Now add chopped tomatoes, salt, and turmeric powder. Saute it.
  5. Then add chopped methi leaves and mix well. Cover and cook for 2-3 minutes till methi become soft and oil is released from the sides of the pan.
  6. Add coriander powder, red chili powder, garam masala, amchur powder, and sugar. Saute it.
  7. Then add besan and roast it on low flame till it slightly changes its color and becomes aromatic.
  8. Now add 2 cups of hot water gradually and mix well. (refer to video)
  9. Cover and cook sabzi on low flame till oil is released from its sides.
  10. Serve hot methi nu lotiyu with bajra roti and fried chilies.

Common questions

Is Thali healthy for kids?

Thali scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on sodium, saturated fat, hfss status.

What age is Thali best for?

It scores highest, 80 out of 100, for 7–9 years and 10–12 years and 13–15 years, and lowest, 70, for 1–3 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

How is the Poshan Score worked out?

Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
