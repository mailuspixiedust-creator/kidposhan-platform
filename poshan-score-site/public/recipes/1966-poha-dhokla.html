Method

Making poha dhokla

  1. In a mixture jar, add poha, rava, and besan. Cover the lid and Grind it into a coarse powder. Do not grind it into fine powder.
  2. Take out dhokla mix into mixing bowl, add sour curd and water gradually and make medium thick and pouring consistency of the batter.
  3. Then add turmeric powder, salt, green chili paste, ginger paste, sugar, and oil. mix well and whisk batter in one direction for 2-3 minutes.
  4. Cover and rest batter for 20-30 minutes.
  5. Meanwhile, add water to the pan. Cover and steam for 5 minutes.
  6. After resting time, if the batter is slightly thick then add some water. Make sure the batter is medium thick and pouring. (Refer to video).
  7. Then add Eno fruit salt & mix in one direction.
  8. Grease the dhokla plate with oil & pour the mixture into the plate.
  9. Steam in idli-dhokla steamer or Kadai for 12-15 minutes on high flame.
  10. Once done take out the plate from the steamer & allow it to cool for 5-10 min.
  11. Cut into the desired shape.
  12. For dhokla tempering- in a tadka pan, add oil, mustard seeds, sesame seeds, chopped green chili, curry leaves, and a pinch of hing. Saute it.
  13. Then add tempering on dhokla and spread it.
  14. Sprinkle some coriander leaves and serve with garlic chutney.

Common questions

Is Poha dhokla healthy for kids?

Poha dhokla scores 80 out of 100 (OK, held by a risk check) for 4–6 years. It passes 8 of the 10 checks and loses points on sodium, being high in fat, sugar or salt.

What age is Poha dhokla best for?

It scores highest, 80 out of 100, for 4–6 years and 7–9 years, and lowest, 70, for 1–3 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

How is the Poshan Score worked out?

Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
