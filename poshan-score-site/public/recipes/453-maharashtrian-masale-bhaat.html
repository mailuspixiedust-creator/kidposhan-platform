Maharashtrian Masale Bhaat

Maharashtrian Masale Bhaat scores 80 out of 100 (OK, held by a risk check) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Go easy on the chilli for under-5s: use less, or leave it out.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons oil (or ghee)
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1 stalk curry leaves
  • 1 teaspoon ginger (grated)
  • 1 green chili (thinly sliced)
  • 1 pinch hing
  • 1 small onion (finely chopped)
  • 2 cups mixed veggies (carrot, potato, green peas)
  • 2-2.5 teaspoons goda masala (I like Kpra brand)
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon red chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon coriander powder
  • salt (to taste)
  • 1 cup basmati rice
  • 1.5 cups water
  • 1 tablespoon ghee
  • 8-10 roasted cashews
  • 2 tablespoons fresh coconut (shredded)
  • 2 tablespoons coriander leaves

Common questions

Is Maharashtrian Masale Bhaat healthy for kids?

Maharashtrian Masale Bhaat scores 80 out of 100 (OK, held by a risk check) for 4–6 years. It passes 8 of the 10 checks and loses points on saturated fat, being high in fat, sugar or salt.

What age is Maharashtrian Masale Bhaat best for?

It scores highest, 100 out of 100, for 7–9 years and 10–12 years and 13–15 years, and lowest, 80, for 1–3 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

How is the Poshan Score worked out?

Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
