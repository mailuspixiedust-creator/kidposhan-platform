Fada ni khichdi

Recipe by Nehas Cook Book

Photo: Nehas Cook Book
⏱ 15 minServes 3VegetarianBreakfastDinnerLunchSchool lunchboxFrom 3 years

Fada ni khichdi scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Go easy on the chilli for under-5s: use less, or leave it out.

Ingredients

  • ½ cup daliya (broken wheat)
  • ¼ cup moong dal
  • 2 tbsp ghee
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • Pinch of hing
  • 1 dry red chilli
  • Few curry leaves
  • ½ tsp ginger paste
  • ¼ tsp turmeric powder
  • 1 tsp chilli powder
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • Salt to taste
  • ½ cup carrot
  • 2 tbsp green peas
  • ½ cup potatoes
  • 1 chopped tomatoes
  • 2.5 cup water

Method

  • Wash and soak daliya and moong dal for 20 minutes. Drain water and keep it aside
  • Turn on Geek robocook on strong sauté mode.
  • Heat ghee or oil. Add mustard seed, cumin seeds, a pinch of hing, dry red chilli, curry leaves and ginger paste. Sauté for a minute.
  • Then add soaked daliya and moong dal mixture. Sauté it for 3 minutes.
  • Add turmeric powder, red chilli powder, garam masala, and salt. Also add chopped carrots, potatoes, peas, and tomatoes. Mix well.
  • Add 2.5 cup water and mix well.
  • Cover the electric pressure cooker with a locking lid.
  • Cancel sauté mode and select the option of khichdi and set the cooker on quick taste or under high pressure.
  • Once the electric pressure cooker beeps and on the display screen you see LO:00, turn off the cooker.
  • Let the pressure release naturally.
  • Open the lid. Fada khichdi is ready.
  • Serve khichdi with raita and papad.

    Common questions

    Is Fada ni khichdi healthy for kids?

    Fada ni khichdi scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on sodium, saturated fat, hfss status.

    What age is Fada ni khichdi best for?

    It scores highest, 100 out of 100, for 10–12 years and 13–15 years and 16–18 years, and lowest, 70, for 1–3 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

    How is the Poshan Score worked out?

    Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
