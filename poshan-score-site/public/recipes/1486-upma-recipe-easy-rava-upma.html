Ingredients

  • 1 cup rava (– finer variety, sooji or cream of wheat or semolina)
  • 2 tablespoons ghee (clarified butter, or oil)
  • 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
  • ½ teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1 teaspoon chana dal (split and husked bengal gram)
  • 1 teaspoon urad dal (split and husked black gram)
  • 10 to 12 cashews (– optional)
  • ⅓ cup or 1 medium-sized onion (– finely chopped)
  • 1 teaspoon or 1 green chilli (– chopped)
  • 1 teaspoon or 1 inch ginger (– finely chopped)
  • 1 sprig curry leaves (or 10 to 12 curry leaves)
  • 2 ½ cups water
  • salt (as required)
  • ½ to 1 teaspoon sugar (or add as required, optional)
  • 2 to 3 tablespoons coriander leaves (– chopped, cilantro)

Is Upma Recipe (Easy Rava Upma) healthy for kids?

Upma Recipe (Easy Rava Upma) scores 80 out of 100 (OK, held by a risk check) for 4–6 years. It passes 8 of the 10 checks and loses points on sodium, being high in fat, sugar or salt.

What age is Upma Recipe (Easy Rava Upma) best for?

It scores highest, 100 out of 100, for 10–12 years and adults, and lowest, 80, for 1–3 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

How is the Poshan Score worked out?

Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
