Korean Beef Bulgogi

How often: Sometimes · high in saturated fat About once a week. The Poshan Score shows how one serving does against a child's daily limits; this line is about how the food is made.

Korean Beef Bulgogi scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup fresh pear, grated (Asian pear, bosc pear or apple works)
  • 1/4 cup soy sauce (or GF liquid aminos)
  • 1 1/2 tablespoons brown sugar (or use coconut sugar, maple syrup, or honey)
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil
  • 3 garlic cloves, pressed or minced
  • 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, grated or minced
  • 1 tablespoon gochujang pepper paste (or sub garlic chili paste)
  • 1 ½ pounds ribeye steak, sirloin, strip loin, or flat iron, thinly sliced (⅛-inch thick). See notes.
  • 1-2 tablespoons high heat oil –peanut oil, avocado oil, wok oil, etc.
  • 2 green onions, finely sliced
  • 2-3 teaspoons sesame seeds, toasted
  • red pepper flakes to taste

Method

  • . In a bowl, mix together grated pear, soy sauce, sugar, sesame oil, garlic, ginger, and gochujang.
  • See notes for slicing your own steak.
  • Place steak strips in the marinade in a sealed container or use a ziplock bag.
  • Marinate for at least 2 hours and up to 24 hours in the fridge.
  • At this point, you could also store up to 3 days in the fridge or freeze for meal prep.
  • Remove the marinated meat from the fridge, let it sit at room temperature for a few minutes before cooking.
  • Shake excess marinade off the steak (save it!) before cooking- this will help it sear vs. steam.
  • Heat oil in a cast iron skillet or grill pan to medium-high heat.
  • See notes for grilling.
  • Sear in batches in a single layer, roughly 2 minutes per side.
  • Avoid moving or over-crowding the pan (if too crowded, the meat will steam rather than sear).
  • We are going for quick and hot for caramelization on the exterior and tender within.
  • If there is leftover marinade, add it to the pan after cooking the meat, simmer it for 30 seconds, then drizzle it over the cooked meat.
  • .
  • Arrange cooked meat on a platter and sprinkle with sliced green onions, toasted sesame seeds, freshly ground black pepper, red pepper flakes, and fresh cilantro.

    Common questions

    Is Korean Beef Bulgogi healthy for kids?

    Korean Beef Bulgogi scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on saturated fat, fibre, being high in fat, sugar or salt.

    What age is Korean Beef Bulgogi best for?

    It scores highest, 90 out of 100, for 7–9 years and 10–12 years and 13–15 years, and lowest, 50, for 1–3 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

    How is the Poshan Score worked out?

    Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
