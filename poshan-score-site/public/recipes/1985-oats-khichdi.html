Oats Khichdi

Recipe by Swasthi's Recipes

Oats Khichdi scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years, worked out for one serving. The same dish scores differently for different ages, because a younger child's daily limit for sugar, salt and saturated fat is smaller.

Go easy on the chilli for under-5s: use less, or leave it out.

Ingredients

  • ½ cup moong dal (yellow split mung lentils)
  • 1 cup rolled oats (or instant)
  • 1 small tomato (deseeded, optional)
  • ½ cup carrots (chopped, 1 small)
  • ½ cup green peas (frozen or fresh)
  • ⅓ cup green beans (10 to 12)
  • ¼ teaspoon red chilli powder (or black pepper)
  • 4 cups water (read notes)
  • 2 tablespoon ghee (or 1 tbsp oil)
  • 1 small bay leaf (optional)
  • ½ teaspoon cumin seeds (jeera)
  • 1 teaspoon ginger (grated or crushed)
  • 1 green chili (slit, deseeded for low heat, optional)
  • ½ teaspoon salt (as needed)
  • ⅛ teaspoon turmeric (haldi)
  • 1 pinch asafoetida (Hing, optional)

Method

Preparation

  1. Rinse moong dal well a few times. Drain the water.
  2. Rinse and chop tomato, carrot, green beans & green chili. Grate ginger.

Common questions

Is Oats Khichdi healthy for kids?

Oats Khichdi scores 70 out of 100 (OK) for 4–6 years. It passes 7 of the 10 checks and loses points on sodium, saturated fat, hfss status.

What age is Oats Khichdi best for?

It scores highest, 100 out of 100, for 10–12 years and 13–15 years and 16–18 years, and lowest, 70, for 1–3 years. The same dish scores differently as a child grows, because the daily limits for sugar, salt and saturated fat grow with them.

How is the Poshan Score worked out?

Ten yes-or-no checks on one serving: added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fibre, protein, preservatives, artificial additives, the first ingredient, and whether it is high in fat, sugar or salt. Each passed check is worth 10 points. The limits come from ICMR-NIN 2020, WHO and FSSAI and change with the age.
