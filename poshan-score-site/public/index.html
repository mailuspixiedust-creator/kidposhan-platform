How we rate

Ten things we check, every time.

The same rubric for every product on the shelf — theirs and ours. Ten yes-or-no checks, each worth 10 points, worked out for the age group (and, from 10 years, boy or girl, man or woman) and one realistic serving. Six are numbers off the label, measured against that group's daily reference. Three are checks on the ingredient list. The tenth fails if the sugar, sodium or saturated-fat check fails. Full citations and thresholds on the Age & Metrics page.

Added Sugar

Yes if one serving stays under 30% of the day's free-sugar limit for that age group (15% for snacks and sweets). The limit is 5% of energy, from WHO, on ICMR-NIN energy.

Sodium

Yes if one serving stays under 30% of the day's sodium (15% for snacks and sweets). ICMR-NIN's adult limit is 2,000 mg; for children we scale it down by energy.

Saturated Fat

Yes if one serving stays under 30% of the day's limit (15% for snacks and sweets). The limit is WHO's under 10% of energy.

Trans Fat

Yes if no hydrogenated oil, vanaspati or margarine is listed. FSSAI caps industrial trans fat at 2% of oils and fats, the same at every age.

Fiber

Yes if one serving gives at least 15% of the day's fibre for that age group, from ICMR-NIN 2020.

Protein

Yes if one serving gives at least 15% of the day's protein for that age group, sex and kind of work, from the ICMR-NIN 2020 RDAs.

Preservatives

Yes if no Class II preservative is named in the ingredients: the ones parents recognise least, checked most.

Artificial Additives

Yes if no artificial colour, flavour enhancer or sweetener is named, by INS number or by name.

Base-Ingredient Integrity

Yes if the first ingredient is a real food, not sugar or a refined flour, starch or fat.

HFSS Status

Yes only if the sugar, sodium and saturated-fat checks all pass. Echoes FSSAI's pending front-of-pack warning for foods high in fat, sugar or salt.
