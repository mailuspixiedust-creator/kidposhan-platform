Age-banded rubric  ·  How to read it

Ten metrics, equal weight, scored per age band.

Six metrics are real numbers off the label measured as a share of an age band's daily reference — added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, fiber and protein. Fiber and protein are credit metrics (more is better); the rest are risk metrics (less is better). Three more are declared/not-declared checks that don't change with age — preservatives, artificial additives, and base-ingredient integrity. The tenth is a composite: whether the product would trip FSSAI's own proposed HFSS warning. A product that fails any single risk metric outright is capped at Caution overall — low fiber or protein cost points but can't trigger that cap on their own. Every number below is cited — sources are listed in full at the bottom of this page.

Step 2

The ten metrics, and how each is measured

Six move with the age band you picked above. Three are the same pass/fail check regardless of age. The tenth is a composite of the other nine, modelled on a real, currently-pending Indian regulation. This is exactly why we don't trust the front of the pack — watch what's printed there against what's actually on the back.

Added Sugar

Scales by age

Read off the "Added Sugars" line, compared against the age band's daily gram limit.

Pass: <10% of daily ideal limit per serving · Partial: <30% · Fail: 30%+
Source: WHO free-sugar guideline [2]
2

Sodium

Scales by age

Read off the "Sodium" line, compared against the age band's daily reference.

Pass: <10% of daily reference per serving · Partial: <30% · Fail: 30%+
Source: NASEM/IOM DRI, used as proxy — ICMR has no pediatric table [4]
3

Saturated Fat

Scales by age

Regulated separately from trans fat — a product can pass the trans-fat check and still be loaded with saturated fat from palm oil, coconut oil, or ghee-heavy formulations.

Daily limit derived as 10% of age-band energy ÷ 9 kcal/g
Source: WHO saturated-fat guidance, <10% of energy [3]
4

Trans Fat / Hydrogenated Oils

Fixed, all ages

The one metric with a real regulatory number that applies uniformly — not age-based, but universal.

FSSAI ceiling: ≤2% of total fat is trans fat, for every packaged food
Source: FSSAI trans-fat regulation [5]
5

Fiber

Credit metric

The first metric that rewards a product for being good, not just penalizes it for being bad.

Pass: serving covers 15%+ of daily fiber need · Partial: 5–15% · Fail: <5%
Source: NASEM/IOM adequate intake, used as proxy [4]
6

Protein

Credit metric

ICMR-NIN 2020 publishes real age-wise protein RDAs — this is the one credit metric with a genuine Indian pediatric reference behind it, not a proxy.

Pass: serving covers 15%+ of daily protein RDA · Partial: 5–15% · Fail: <5%
Source: ICMR-NIN 2020 protein RDA by age [1]
7

Preservatives

Fixed, all ages

Presence/absence of FSSAI Class II preservatives (e.g. INS 200–203, 211, 220–228) by name.

0 preservatives = pass · 1 = partial · 2+ = fail
Source: FSSAI Food Products Standards and Food Additives Regulations, 2011 [6]
8

Artificial Additives

Fixed, all ages

Flavour enhancers and synthetic colours by INS number (e.g. 627, 631, 635, 150d, 319/320).

0 codes = pass · 1–2 = partial · 3+ = fail
Source: FSSAI Food Products Standards and Food Additives Regulations, 2011 [6]
9

Base-Ingredient Integrity

Fixed, all ages

Whether the first ingredient on the pack is actually the food it's named after.

Named ingredient is #1 by weight = pass
Judgment-based read of ingredient order — no single regulation covers this directly
10

HFSS Status

Composite flag

Not a new nutrient — a derived flag modelled on FSSAI's own proposed rule. On 29 August 2026, FSSAI told the Supreme Court of India it plans a front-of-pack red warning for products high in fat, sugar, or salt (HFSS), with thresholds from the Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2024. Their Phase I proposal flags products high in 2+ of the three; a later Phase II would flag any one. We apply the stricter Phase II standard now.

Fail (0 pts) if sugar, sodium, or saturated fat scores a fail on its own — any one is enough
Source: FSSAI compliance affidavit, Supreme Court of India, W.P.(C) No. 437/2024, 29 Aug 2026 [7]

References

Every number on this page, sourced

  1. [1] ICMR-NIN, "Nutrient Requirements for Indians — Recommended Dietary Allowances and Estimated Average Requirements," 2020. Source for protein RDA by age and the pediatric energy reference figures used throughout.
  2. [2] WHO, "Guideline: Sugars intake for adults and children," 2015. Free sugars should be under 10% of total energy, with additional benefit below 5% — converted to grams here at 4 kcal/g.
  3. [3] WHO saturated fat guidance (WHO/FAO expert consultations on fats and fatty acids). Saturated fat under 10% of total energy — converted to grams here at 9 kcal/g.
  4. [4] NASEM (National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine) / Institute of Medicine, Dietary Reference Intakes. Used as a proxy for pediatric sodium and fiber references, since ICMR-NIN 2020 does not publish an Indian-specific age-wise breakdown for either.
  5. [5] FSSAI, Food Safety and Standards (Prohibition and Restrictions on Sales) Regulations. Trans fat capped at ≤2% of total oils and fats in any food product, all ages, all categories.
  6. [6] FSSAI, Food Safety and Standards (Food Products Standards and Food Additives) Regulations, 2011. Governs permitted preservatives (Class II) and additives (colours, flavour enhancers) by INS number.
  7. [7] FSSAI compliance affidavit, Supreme Court of India, W.P.(C) No. 437/2024 — 3S and Our Health Society vs Union of India, filed 29 August 2026. Proposes a front-of-pack red hexagon warning for foods high in added saturated fat, added sugar, and/or salt (HFSS), with thresholds to be drawn from the Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2024 (ICMR-NIN). This is a live, pending proceeding — not yet a notified regulation.

What's a real citation vs. a judgment call: metrics 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 10 are numbers compared against a published daily reference. Metrics 4, 7 and 8 are declared/not-declared checks against a named regulation. Metric 9 (Base-Ingredient Integrity) is the one metric on this page without a single governing regulation — it's our own read of ingredient order, applied consistently.
