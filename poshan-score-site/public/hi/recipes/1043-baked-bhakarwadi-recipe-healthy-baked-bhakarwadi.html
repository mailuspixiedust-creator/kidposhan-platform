सामग्री

  • 1/2 cup whole wheat flour (gehun ka atta)
  • 1/4 cup besan (gram flour)
  • 1/8 tsp haldi powder (turmeric powder)
  • 1/4 tsp red chilli powder
  • salt (namak) to taste
  • 3/4 tbsp oil
  • 1/4 cup grated dry coconut (kopra)
  • 2 tbsp sesame seeds (til)
  • 1 tbsp poppy seeds (khus-khus)
  • 3/4 tsp dhaniya powder (coriander powder)
  • 3/4 tsp cumin seeds (jeera) powder
  • 1/2 tbsp coarsely fennel seeds (saunf) powder
  • 1/2 tsp grated ginger (adrak)
  • 1/2 tbsp red chilli powder
  • 3/4 tsp dried mango powder (amchur)
  • 1/8 tsp asafoetida (hing)
  • 1/2 tbsp sugar
  • 1/2 tbsp besan (gram flour)

विधि

  • What is baked bhakarwadi made of ? See below image of list of ingredients for baked bhakarwadi.
  • In a deep bowl put 1/2 cup whole wheat flour (gehun ka atta).
  • Add 1/4 cup besan (bengal gram flour).
  • Add 1/8 tsp turmeric powder (haldi).
  • Add 1/4 tsp chilli powder.
  • Add salt to taste. We added 1/8th tsp salt.
  • Add 3/4 tbsp oil.
  • Gradually add enough water to make a semi stiff dough. We added 1 tablespoon water and then added gradually 3 1/2 tablespoons water while making the dough.
  • Knead into a semi-stiff dough.
  • Cover with a lid and keep aside.
  • Heat a broad non-stick pan.
  • Add 1/4 cup grated dry coconut (kopra).
  • Dry roast on a medium flame for 1 minute or till the coconut turns light brown in colour.
  • Remove it in a deep bowl and keep aside.
  • Add 2 tbsp sesame seeds (til) into the same pan.
  • Dry roast on a medium flame for 1 minute or till it turns light brown in colour.
  • Remove it in the bowl containing roasted dry coconut and keep aside.
  • Add 1 tbsp poppy seeds (khus-khus) into the same pan.
  • Dry roast on a medium flame for 30 seconds or till they turn light brown in colour.
  • Remove it in the bowl containing roasted dry coconut and sesame seeds. Keep aside to cool for 1 to 2 minutes.
  • Add 3/4 tsp coriander (dhania) powder.
  • Add 3/4 tsp cumin seeds (jeera) powder.
  • Add 1/2 tbsp coarsely powdered fennel seeds (saunf).
  • Add 1/2 tsp grated ginger (adrak).
  • Add 1/2 tbsp chilli powder.
  • Add 1/8 tsp turmeric powder (haldi).
  • Add 3/4 tsp dried mango powder (amchur).
  • Add 1/8 tsp asafoetida (hing).
  • Add 1/2 tbsp sugar.
  • Add salt to taste. We added 1/3rd tsp salt.
  • Mix well and put in a mixer.
  • Blend it in a mixer to a coarse powder.
  • Transfer blended powder to a bowl.
  • Add 1/2 tbsp besan (bengal gram flour).
  • Mix masala for bhakarawadi well. Keep aside.
  • Divide the masala into 4 equal portions.
  • Flatten the dough.
  • Roll a portion of the dough into 200 mm. (8”) diameter circle using very little flour for rolling if required.
  • Place a portion of the masala over it.
  • Pat masala lightly while leaving a little space at the circumference. Use a spoon to help you do that.
  • Gently roll over the masala evenly using a rolling pin, to ensure that the masala sticks well to the dough circle.
  • Spread a little water at the circumference of the dough circle which doesn’t have any masala.
  • Immediately start rolling very tightly from one end of the circle towards the other end to form a cylindrical roll while pinching the masala inwards simultaneously.
  • Roll stuffed dough from one end to another end to form a cylindrical roll.
  • Gently press all over the cylindrical roll evenly so that the masala further sticks to the dough circle very well.
  • Trim a small portion from both the sides of the cylindrical roll and discard them.
  • Now cut it into 16 to 18 equal roundels using a knife and separate out each roundel very well.
  • Press each rolled bhakarwadi in a slanting manner using your palm.
  • Bhakarwadi pressed in a slanting way.
  • Place them on a baking tray lined with parchment paper.
  • Bake them in a pre-heated oven at 180°c (360°f) for 10 minutes.
  • Turn them over with your fingers.
  • Bake again for 10 minutes at the same temperature.
  • Roll a portion of the dough into 200 mm. (8”) diameter circle using very little flour for rolling if required.
  • Pat masala lightly while leaving a little space at the circumference. Use a spoon to help you do that.
  • Gently roll over the masala evenly using a rolling pin, to ensure that the masala sticks well to the dough circle.
  • Immediately start rolling very tightly from one end of the circle towards the other end to form a cylindrical roll while pinching the masala inwards simultaneously.
